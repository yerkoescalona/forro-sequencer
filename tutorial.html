Tutorial

Learn how to use the Forró Sequencer in five steps. No music theory required — just click and play.

01

Pick an instrument

Choose one of the three traditional forró percussion instruments:

  • Zabumba — the bass drum that drives the rhythm. Two tracks: maceta (mallet, open/closed) and bacalhau (stick, normal/accent).
  • Pandeiro — the frame drum. One track with five sounds: off, xi, tum (open), tũ (closed), and tapa (slap).
  • Triângulo — the triangle. Two sounds: closed (muted) and open (ringing).

Select TODOS / ENSEMBLE to hear all three instruments playing together.

02

Choose a rhythm

Click one of the preset buttons to load a traditional pattern into the grid. The sequencer starts with Baião loaded by default.

Available rhythms: Baião, Xote, Forró, Xaxado, Coco, and Rastapé. Each preset also sets the tempo to its traditional BPM.

All rhythm names are Portuguese — they refer to specific dance and music styles from northeastern Brazil. They are not translated.

03

Press Play

Hit the TOCAR / PLAY button to start the rhythm. You'll see the step indicator moving across the grid and hear the pattern.

  • PARAR / STOP — stops playback
  • LIMPAR / CLEAR — erases the current pattern
  • TAP TEMPO — tap the button repeatedly to set the BPM by feel

You can also adjust BPM precisely with the slider or the +/− buttons.

04

Edit the pattern

Click any cell in the grid to cycle through the available sounds for that track. Each click advances to the next state:

  • Zabumba maceta: off → open → closed → off
  • Zabumba bacalhau: off → normal → accent → off
  • Pandeiro: off → xi → tum → tũ → tapa → off
  • Triângulo: off → closed → open → off

The legend below the grid shows the color coding for each sound.

Editing a cell clears the active preset highlight — your pattern is now custom. Load a preset again to reset.

05

Add bars & change resolution

Use + COMPASSO / + BAR to add bars for longer patterns. Remove with − COMPASSO / − BAR.

Toggle grid resolution:

  • 4+4 — 8 steps per bar (eighth notes). The default, good for most patterns.
  • 12+12 — 24 steps per bar (triplet subdivisions). Higher resolution for advanced patterns and swing feels.
The rhythms of forró

Baião

~92 BPM

The foundational rhythm of forró, created by Luiz Gonzaga and Humberto Teixeira in the 1940s.

Xote

~76 BPM

The slowest forró rhythm, derived from the European schottische.

Forró

~100 BPM

The genre's namesake rhythm. Faster than baião with a busier bacalhau pattern.

Xaxado

~110 BPM

Originally a war dance of the cangaceiros (bandits) of northeastern Brazil.

Coco

~120 BPM

A circle-dance rhythm from the coast of northeastern Brazil.

Rastapé

~140 BPM

The fastest forró style — high-energy and exhilarating.

Tips

Quick-start recipe

Select ENSEMBLE → pick BAIÃO → press PLAY. You'll hear the full trio instantly.
